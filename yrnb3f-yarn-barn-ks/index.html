Yarn Barn of Kansas has been supplying knitting, weaving, spinning, and crocheting materials since 1971 — a specialty fiber arts shop built for the long haul, in a college town that's kept its needles moving for more than fifty years.

Four crafts, one storefront

Everything on the shelves exists to serve one of these four practices — yarn and tools chosen for people who actually make things with their hands.
Knitting

Two needles, one continuous strand, loop drawn through loop — sweaters, socks, hats, and blankets built row by row from real yarn, not synthetic filler.

Weaving

Warp and weft cross on a loom until they lock into cloth — rugs, wall hangings, and yardage, the oldest way there is to turn yarn into fabric.

Spinning

Raw fiber twisted on a wheel or a spindle into the very yarn the other three crafts start with — where the material itself gets made.

Crocheting

One hook, one loop at a time — fabric built without a loom or a second needle, stitch by stitch, as old as the craft it sits beside.

Our Heritage

Half a century of supplying Kansas fiber artists

55+Years on Massachusetts Street
“Yarn Barn of Kansas has been supplying knitting, weaving, spinning, and crocheting materials since 1971.” The shop's own founding line

More than fifty years in, the shop is still doing the same job it opened to do: stocking real yarn, real looms, and real know-how for a craft community that hasn't gone anywhere. In a college town built around the University of Kansas, that kind of staying power is its own kind of credential — no gimmicks, just decades of showing up for knitters, weavers, spinners, and crocheters.
