Welcome to The Capital of Cheese

Fromagination is an artisan cheese shop on Madison's Capitol Square — artisanal cheeses and perfect companions, cut to order by cheesemongers who know every wheel by name.

Visit the Shop

For about seven months of the year, the Dane County Farmers Market — one of the largest producer-only farmers markets in the country — fills the square right outside our door every Saturday morning. Step inside and the market continues: hundreds of cheeses from Wisconsin, across the U.S., and abroad, hand-cut in an Old World shop built with recycled materials and low-energy fixtures.

Why Wisconsin

A Cheese Heritage Worth Showcasing

Three facts that explain why Wisconsin cheese deserves its own showcase.

01

The only state that requires it

Wisconsin is the only U.S. state that requires a licensed cheesemaker on-site during the manufacturing of every single vat of cheese — a standard upheld by the Wisconsin Master Cheesemaker Program, established by UW-Madison's Center for Dairy Research, UW Extension, and Dairy Farmers of Wisconsin.

02

A dairy state by 1915

In 1899, 90% of Wisconsin farms kept dairy cows. By 1915, Wisconsin had become the nation's leading dairy producer — a lead built in no small part on UW Professor Stephen Babcock's invention of the butterfat test.

03

Two cheeses born here

Brick and Colby — two distinct American cheese styles — were both invented in Wisconsin. That inventive streak is still what we look for in every cheesemaker we carry.

Since 2007

Meet the Cheesemongers

The people behind the counter, and the man who started it all.

Ken MonteleoneOwner & Creative Director, founded Fromagination in 2007

Ken opened Fromagination believing that Wisconsin cheese, the state's cheese-making heritage, and its artisan food movement deserved a real showcase — not just a shelf. Nearly two decades later, the shop has carried hundreds of cheeses from Wisconsin, the rest of the U.S., and abroad, including Vermont, Minnesota, California, Italy, France, England, and the Netherlands.

Our cheesemongers come from farming, culinary work, bartending, retail, grocery, and catering backgrounds. They lead tastings and classes, and show up at weddings, workshops, and street fairs across Madison — because the best way to talk about cheese is in person, sample in hand.
